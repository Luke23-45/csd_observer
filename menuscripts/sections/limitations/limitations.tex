\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The benchmark uses three synthetic dynamical systems. These systems were chosen
to represent distinct bifurcation mechanisms (fold, Hopf, period-doubling), but
they do not cover the full range of transitions encountered in physical,
biological, or engineered systems. Performance on these three systems should not
be assumed to transfer to other bifurcation types or to empirical data without
further validation.

The finalized benchmark reports two observer variants. During development,
additional configurations were explored---EWS-augmented inputs, auxiliary
spectral heads, parity-aware observers---but these were not run across the
complete seed and system grid. The main comparison is therefore between
Kalman-BCE and Kalman-LSTM-Spec only. A broader set of learned and classical
methods would strengthen the comparison.

Threshold selection affects detection-time and false-positive-rate summaries.
The Youden-based rule used here (Section~\ref{sec:metrics:threshold}) is one
reasonable choice; a different calibration procedure could shift the DT and FPR
values without changing the EW-AUC ranking. Conclusions that depend on threshold
crossings---particularly detection time---should be interpreted with this
sensitivity in mind.

The patient-count sweep covers 100 to 500 trajectories. Performance at larger
or smaller scales may differ. The Hopf system in particular shows unstable
threshold behavior at 200~patients (Section~\ref{sec:results:sweep}),
suggesting that the patient-count range does not fully resolve the data
requirements for all systems.

All trajectories have fixed length~200 with a single observation noise level.
Varying the sequence length, noise regime, or bifurcation timing could change
the relative ranking of the two methods.
