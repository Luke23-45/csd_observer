\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper compared two learned Kalman observer variants---Kalman-BCE and
Kalman-LSTM-Spec---across fold, Hopf, and logistic bifurcation systems, five
patient counts, ten seeds per setting, and three evaluation metrics.

The results show that observer performance is system- and metric-dependent.
Kalman-LSTM-Spec achieves higher early-warning AUC on fold and Hopf systems and
produces sharper score transitions, but it does not dominate Kalman-BCE on
detection time or false-positive rate. On the logistic system, both methods
discriminate perfectly and differ only in false-positive control. The relative
ranking also shifts with patient count: LSTM-Spec benefits more from additional
training data on fold and Hopf, while logistic performance saturates early.

The contribution is a controlled comparison, not a universal detector. Learned
observer design for early-warning detection should be treated as a
regime-dependent modeling problem, where the choice of architecture and
regularization is informed by the dynamical setting and the operational metric
that matters most.
