\begin{abstract}
Early-warning methods for critical transitions are often evaluated on a
single dynamical regime, leaving open the question of whether the learned
behavior transfers across bifurcation mechanisms. We study this question
with two learned Kalman observer variants---a binary-cross-entropy observer
(Kalman-BCE) and a spectral recurrent variant (Kalman-LSTM-Spec)---on
synthetic fold, Hopf, and logistic systems. The benchmark varies the
training-set size from 100 to 500 trajectories and aggregates over ten
random seeds per setting. At 500 trajectories, the spectral recurrent
observer achieves higher early-warning AUC on fold ($0.903$ vs.\ $0.827$)
and Hopf ($0.823$ vs.\ $0.617$), while both methods reach perfect AUC on
logistic dynamics. However, the BCE observer produces earlier detections on
fold and lower false-positive rates on fold and logistic. The relative
ranking also shifts with training-set size. No single observer dominates
across all systems, metrics, and data sizes. These results indicate that
observer design for early-warning detection should be treated as a
regime-dependent modeling problem rather than a search for a universally
transferable detector.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} critical transitions; early-warning signals; Kalman observer; bifurcation detection; spectral regularization; dynamical systems
